01 — The BriefChoosing a bank that sits at the intersection of every risk vector that matters now.

The assignment was open-ended: identify a publicly listed financial institution, systematically map its material enterprise risks, and present the analysis as a professional risk dashboard grounded in recognised frameworks. I chose DBS Bank Ltd — Southeast Asia's largest bank by assets (SGD 680B+ in 2023), operating across 19 markets, and one of the region's most aggressive digital transformers.

My focus risk was cybersecurity and data privacy. The rationale was material and current: DBS suffered a documented breach in April 2025 affecting approximately 8,200 customers through a third-party vendor (The Straits Times, 2025). At the same time, 51% of financial services executives globally now rank cybersecurity as their top compliance priority (PwC, 2025). The topic was traceable through public disclosures, quantifiable, and directly relevant to how a modern bank actually loses value.

Sound risk management is ultimately about making deliberate, boundary-aware trade-offs, not pursuing unconstrained growth.

02 — ApproachThree frameworks, applied deliberately rather than decoratively.

Rather than default to a single framework, I built the analysis through a stacked approach — each framework doing a specific job in the pipeline from identification to monitoring.

Structure
ISO 31000:2018

Set the identification, assessment, and treatment sequence. Anchored the risk register and heat map to a defensible international standard.

Strategy
COSO ERM (2017)

Linked risk categories to strategic value creation. Grounded the KRI dashboard and escalation matrix in enterprise governance logic.

Thresholds
DBS Risk Appetite

Used DBS's own published Risk Appetite Statement to derive KRI thresholds and tie monitoring back to the bank's declared tolerance.

Sources triangulated across the DBS 2023 Annual Report, DBS Sustainability Report 2023, Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) Technology Risk Management Guidelines, MAS Notice 655 on Cyber Hygiene, NIST SP 800-207 (Zero Trust Architecture), TCFD (2021), and the PwC Global Compliance Survey 2025.

03 — Risks IdentifiedEight material risks across four categories, ranked by likelihood × impact.

Each risk was assessed on a 1–5 likelihood scale and a 1–5 impact scale, giving a composite score. The weighted portfolio exposure came out to 11.9 / 25 — approximately 56% of the maximum possible score. Cybersecurity was the only risk classified critical.

05 — MitigationRecommendations tied to real regulatory anchors, not generic best practice.

Each recommendation was linked back to a specific standard so it could survive a governance review — MAS TRM, ISO 27001, NIST SP 800-207, TCFD. A representative selection:

Emerging
  • Zero Trust architecture + 24/7 AI-driven SOC (aligned with NIST SP 800-207).
  • Enterprise encryption and data governance per MAS TRM Guidelines.
  • TCFD-aligned climate scenario analysis for stranded asset exposure.
Operational
  • Multi-cloud resilience and DR drills to reduce single-vendor concentration.
  • Application-level circuit breakers to contain outages.
  • Continuous vendor security assessments aligned to ISO 27001.
Financial
  • AI-driven underwriting and portfolio surveillance for SME segments.
  • Dynamic asset-liability matching and stress testing.
  • Proactive loan restructuring in rate-stressed cohorts.
Strategic
  • Expand API banking and super-app ecosystem to fend off BigTech entry.
  • Diversify regional operations to hedge US–China exposure.
  • Quarterly geopolitical scenario planning at board level.

06 — What This Project PracticedThe skills the analysis actually forced me to develop.

Framework synthesis

Applying ISO 31000, COSO ERM, and DBS's own RAF as a stack rather than choosing one — each doing a different job in the pipeline.

Regulatory research

Triangulating across MAS notices, TCFD, NIST, and PwC industry data to defend every recommendation with a citable anchor.

Quantitative scoring

Building a defensible likelihood × impact matrix and weighted portfolio score from qualitative disclosures.

Information design

Rebuilding the analysis as a heat map, KRI panel, and trend chart designed for a board-level scan, not just a report body.

Consulting-style writing

21-page eBook structured as executive summary → identification → dashboard → mitigation → reflection, with a print-ready dark aesthetic.

Cross-domain thinking

Grounding a finance topic in the technical vernacular (Zero Trust, multi-cloud DR, TCFD, MAS TRM) recruiters at global banks expect fluency in.
